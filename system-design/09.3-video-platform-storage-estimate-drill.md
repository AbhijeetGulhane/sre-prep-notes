# Video Platform — Storage Estimate Drill (Cold, No Notes)

*Companion to [09-video-platform.md](./09-video-platform.md). A standalone cold capacity-estimation exercise — worked without referencing doc 09's own numbers — then reconciled against them afterward. Given: 500 hrs video/min uploaded, avg 4K 1-hr video = 10GB, resolutions stored: 1080p + 720p + 480p.*

---

## 1. Per-resolution storage size, derived from the one given data point

4K (3840×2160) = 10 GB/hr, given. Each resolution step down is a geometric relationship in pixel count, assumed to scale storage size roughly proportionally:
- 1080p (1920×1080) has exactly 1/4 the pixels of 4K (each dimension halved).
- 720p and 480p each have roughly 4/9 the pixels of the tier above (each dimension scaled by ~2/3).

```
4K:    10 GB/hr           (given)
1080p:  2.5 GB/hr          (10 ÷ 4)
720p:   1.11 GB/hr         (2.5 × 4/9)
480p:   0.49 GB/hr         (1.11 × 4/9)
```

---

## 2. The assumption worth stating explicitly before computing anything further

The prompt names three retained resolutions (1080p+720p+480p) but doesn't say whether the original 4K master is also kept. Rather than silently picking one, compute both:

```
(a) 3 renditions only, no 4K master retained:   2.5 + 1.11 + 0.49 = 4.1 GB/hr
(b) 3 renditions + retained 4K master:          10 + 2.5 + 1.11 + 0.49 = 14.1 GB/hr
```

---

## 3. Annualizing the ingestion rate

```
Minutes/year: 60 × 24 × 365 = 525,600
Hours of footage/year: 500 hrs/min × 525,600 min/year = 262,800,000 hours/year
```

---

## 4. Total storage per year, before replication

```
(a) 262,800,000 hrs × 4.1 GB/hr  ≈ 1,077,480,000 GB ≈ 1.08 EB/year
(b) 262,800,000 hrs × 14.1 GB/hr ≈ 3,705,480,000 GB ≈ 3.71 EB/year
```

---

## 5. With a 3x replication factor (standard durability assumption, consistent with this repo's treatment elsewhere)

```
(a) ≈ 3.24 EB/year  (no 4K master retained)
(b) ≈ 11.1 EB/year  (4K master retained)
```

---

## 6. Reconciling against doc 09's own figure

Doc 09 landed on **1.62 PB/day raw** (≈591 PB/year), derived from a **blended 5 Mbps average bitrate across the full mix of upload quality tiers** — not a 4K-only assumption. Converting that bitrate into the same GB/hour terms used above: 5 Mbps → **2.25 GB/hour**, versus this drill's 10 GB/hour 4K-source figure — roughly a **4.4x gap**, which matches the ratio between the two bitrate assumptions almost exactly (10 ÷ 2.25 ≈ 4.44).

**The two estimates aren't in tension with each other — they're answering different questions.** Doc 09 assumed a realistic *mixed* upload population (most real-world uploads aren't 4K source quality); this drill assumed every single upload is 4K source, a deliberately more aggressive, upper-bound scenario. Both are internally consistent given their own stated assumption.

---

## 7. The actual takeaway

**The number that matters in an interview isn't which specific EB/PB figure is "correct" — it's being able to name which assumption drove which answer, out loud, before the interviewer has to ask.** Given "avg 4K video = 10GB" as an input, the discipline being tested is deriving the resolution-tier ratios cleanly and multiplying through — not landing on one canonical total. Any of 1.08 EB, 3.71 EB, 3.24 EB, or 11.1 EB/year is a defensible answer, contingent on the (explicitly stated) retention and replication assumptions behind it.
