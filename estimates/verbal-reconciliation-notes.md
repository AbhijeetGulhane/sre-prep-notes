# Verbal Reconciliation Notes — "If Asked" Only

**Rule for both items below: verbal only, never in writing. Never on a resume,
never on LinkedIn, never in an email. These live in your head for screens and
interviews, and come out only if a recruiter or interviewer raises the topic.**

---

## 1. Title reconciliation (Production Engineer vs. Service Engineer)

**On the resume / LinkedIn:** "Production Engineer" (or "Systems Engineer" on the
Google variant) — the accurate functional title.

**HR title of record (Workday):** "Service Engineer."

**If asked about title / if it surfaces in verification:**
> "My functional role was Production Engineer — that's the work I actually did.
> My HR title of record is Service Engineer; the internal HR taxonomy just used a
> different label for the same position."

**Guardrails:**
- On any LEGAL/VERIFICATION document (background-check forms, official applications
  asking for "exact title as it appears with your employer"): use **Service Engineer**.
  Exactness is the whole point on those.
- On marketing documents (resume, LinkedIn): functional title is fine and normal.
- If there's a natural moment in a recruiter screen, front-foot it casually — say it
  once, early, and it can never resurface as a "gotcha."

## 3. L3 → L2 move (on-call escalation tier to hands-on AVP role)

**Never volunteered. Verbal only, if asked "why did you move off the L3 rotation"
or about the transition.**

**The curated ~20-second answer:**
> "After about two years on the L3 global on-call rotation, I wanted deeper
> hands-on infrastructure work. L3 only handled escalated incidents — we didn't
> own patching or day-to-day server operations. I moved into an AVP role on the
> L2 side specifically to get that: patching cycles, hardware and OS fault
> handling, first-response triage on major incidents, and exposure across the
> whole estate instead of a project-specific scope. It was a deliberate move
> toward broader, deeper infra ownership."

**Fallback if an interviewer who knows Barclays presses ("but L2 is below L3?"):**
> "In the operations tier, yes — I traded escalation-tier seniority for hands-on
> breadth, which is what I was after."

**Delivery rules:**
- LEAD with the motivation (wanting deeper hands-on work), never with "level-down."
- Do NOT volunteer "it was a level-down in the ops hierarchy." True, but planting it
  creates a frame you then have to un-plant. Only confirm if directly pressed.
- END on the gain (broader/deeper infra ownership), not the trade-off.
- Keep the L2-vs-L3 mechanics compressed — one distinction (L3 = escalation-only, no
  patching/hands-on) is enough; don't recite the full internal taxonomy.
- The AVP title bump is honest and in the answer, but don't lean on it.

**Guardrails:**
- Resume shows Barclays as ONE continuous block — this internal move is invisible by
  default. This script is the fallback if a question gets past that.

---

**Drill all three as a set before any recruiter screen.** All are low-risk when
prepared, only risky when they surface unexpectedly. Common thread: lead with the
real motivation or the functional truth, never with the internal-leveling artifact.
